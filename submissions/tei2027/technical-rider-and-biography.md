# Identity Machine Technical Rider

Artist: Duyên (Anna) Lê  
School of Art, Texas Tech University  
Contact: duyele@ttu.edu

## Presentation

Proposed format: a facilitated interactive installation with one participant at a time and a small surrounding audience. The participant is photographed, optionally describes an imagined self, wears an EEG headband for a short reading, and encounters a generated portrait and film. An operator assists with the wearable, explains interpretation and consent, and resets the station.

The following dimensions, timings, and capacities are planning estimates, not measured deployment results. Equipment availability and the conference floor plan remain to be confirmed by the artist and organisers.

## Space and Furniture

Request approximately 2 m × 2 m for the station, with additional circulation space outside this footprint. Avoid obstructing an accessible route. Preferred furniture is one stable table approximately 120 cm × 60 cm, one participant chair, and one operator chair. Final height, reach, and clearance should be checked for seated and wheelchair access. A compact side table for cleaning supplies is useful but not essential.

Place the camera and display so a seated participant can frame their face without adopting a fixed standing posture. Adjust the screen angle to prioritise the participant's view. Spectators should stand behind or beside the operator, with participation images visible to them only under the agreed display permission. No dedicated projection surface, enclosure, darkness, or acoustic isolation is required.

## Equipment

Artist-provided equipment proposed for the installation:

- A laptop with a tested browser supporting camera access and the intended Muse Bluetooth connection.
- A compatible Muse EEG headband, charger, and charging cable; a spare headband if available.
- A camera, either integrated or USB, suitable for a clear frontal photograph.
- A 24–32 inch display, adjustable stand, and video cable; the laptop display can serve as the minimum configuration.
- Power adapters, a venue-approved power strip, cable protection, and spare connection cables.
- Manufacturer-compatible headband cleaning supplies and printed interpretation/consent information.
- Hosted application access and an artist-funded generation account.

Requested from organisers: a table, two chairs, nearby power, and network access. A screen is helpful only if confirmed; the plan should not depend on unconfirmed venue equipment.

## Power and Network

One standard mains connection should serve the laptop, display, and charging equipment through a suitable power strip. Confirm actual device ratings and Portugal-compatible adapters before travel; a provisional allowance of 250 W is for planning only.

Reliable outbound HTTPS connectivity is necessary for portrait generation, video generation, and status polling. The wearable connection uses local Bluetooth. Check that conference network restrictions permit the required services. This is not an offline generation system. The artist should budget generation credits and verify service availability immediately before the event.

## Lighting and Sound

Ordinary indoor lighting is sufficient if the camera can capture a clear face. Avoid direct backlighting. A small adjustable light may be supplied by the artist if needed and permitted. The current film generation is silent and playback muted. The installation requires no speakers and can share an exhibition room with other works.

## Session and Capacity

Allow a provisional 5–10 minutes per participant for explanation, fitting, photography, sensing, generation, viewing, and reset. This estimate is subject to an end-to-end rehearsal and variable service delays. Default sensing is approximately 26.5 seconds including settling, baseline, and reading. It is not the total session time.

Default output targets four eight-second video segments. A one-segment configuration can reduce service use and waiting when appropriate. If a later segment fails, available clips remain viewable; if video fails entirely, the portrait remains available. These code paths require live verification before claiming exhibition robustness. Never describe a shorter output or recorded example as a successful full live session.

## Installation and Deinstallation

Reserve a proposed 60–90 minutes for equipment placement, network checks, Bluetooth pairing, camera framing, generation rehearsal, display adjustment, privacy configuration, and cable safety. Allow approximately 30 minutes for deinstallation, removal of equipment, and agreed cleanup of local data. These are artist estimates rather than conference-assigned access times.

The artist or an instructed facilitator should remain at the station during participation. Clean the headband between visitors according to manufacturer guidance. Confirm a practical charging and cleaning schedule before exhibition.

## Failure and Alternative Participation

If connection or sensor contact fails, explain the issue and offer a retry, an explicitly labelled simulated demonstration, or a consented prerecorded example. Neither simulation nor playback substitutes for live physiological participation. If external generation services are unavailable, pause live sessions and show only material for which exhibition permission has been obtained. Visitors may watch or decline the wearable without being described as live EEG participants.

## Privacy and Interpretation Plan

Before exhibition, implement and test a participant explanation covering photography, signal collection, external processing, optional retention, and optional public display. Obtain separate permission for documentation or research. The existing public archive endpoint does not itself provide this workflow and must remain disabled until appropriate choices and retention/deletion procedures are verified. Audit the current logging routes and provider retention before deciding what can accurately be promised.

Keep the signal-source label visible. Identify mood-like labels as artistic vocabulary and disclose the default beauty treatment. Offer an unretouched portrait configuration. Explain that the generated result neither reads thoughts nor predicts the future. Permit withdrawal before recording and define what withdrawal can accomplish after external processing.

## Artist Biography

Duyên (Anna) Lê is an artist and PhD student in Fine Arts at Texas Tech University. Born and raised in Vietnam, she moved to the United States at eighteen. Her practice examines identity as a changing process shaped by memory, performance, technology, and relationships with others and the environments we inhabit. Working across interactive installation, sculpture, creative coding, and emerging technologies, she creates participatory experiences that invite reflection on the selves we construct and negotiate. Identity Machine extends this inquiry through bodily sensing and AI-generated self-representation.

## Optional Ethics Statement

Identity Machine is presented as an artistic installation, not a diagnostic or psychological assessment. EEG features influence authored visual descriptions; they do not establish a participant's thoughts, emotions, or identity. The proposed exhibition will explain this translation before participation and distinguish live sensing from simulation. Consent to processing a photograph and generating media will be separate from permission to retain or publicly exhibit the result. External service processing and applicable retention will be disclosed. No participant study or ethics approval is claimed in this submission. Any future research activity will require its own consent and applicable institutional review.
